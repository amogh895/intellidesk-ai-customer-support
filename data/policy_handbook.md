# NorthBridge Insurance — Policy Handbook

## Personal Auto Insurance Policy — Detailed Clauses


Clause 1 — Liability Coverage (Bodily Injury and Property Damage) The Insurer will pay damages for bodily injury or property damage for which any insured becomes legally responsible because of a covered auto accident. Limits are typically expressed as split limits such as $100,000 per person / $300,000 per accident for bodily injury and $50,000 per accident for property damage (100/300/50), or as a combined single limit. The Insurer also provides a legal defense at its own expense, in addition to the limits of liability. Defense costs do not reduce the coverage available for damages.

Clause 2 — Collision Coverage Collision coverage pays for direct and accidental loss to the covered auto caused by its impact with another vehicle or object, or by the vehicle overturning, regardless of fault. Payment is made at the actual cash value (ACV) or cost to repair, whichever is less, minus the deductible shown on the Declarations Page (commonly ₹25000, ₹50000, or $1,000). Collision coverage requires a separately elected premium and applies only to listed vehicles.

Clause 3 — Comprehensive (Other Than Collision) Coverage Comprehensive coverage pays for loss to the covered auto from causes other than collision, including fire, theft, vandalism, falling objects, hail, flood, windstorm, riot, contact with animals, and glass breakage. A separate, often lower, deductible may apply. Glass-only claims may be settled with no deductible where state law or the endorsement provides.

Clause 4 — Uninsured Motorist (UM) Coverage If an insured suffers bodily injury caused by an owner or operator of an uninsured motor vehicle, the Insurer will pay compensatory damages the insured is legally entitled to recover. This includes hit-and-run accidents where the responsible party cannot be identified. Limits mirror the Bodily Injury liability limits unless the insured has rejected or reduced them in writing where permitted.

Clause 5 — Underinsured Motorist (UIM) Coverage When the at-fault driver carries liability limits lower than the insured's damages, UIM coverage pays the difference up to the UIM limit. This coverage applies only after the at-fault party's limits have been exhausted and is subject to state-specific offset and stacking rules.

Clause 6 — Medical Payments Coverage (MedPay) MedPay covers reasonable and necessary medical and funeral expenses incurred by the insured and passengers as a result of a covered accident, regardless of fault, up to the elected limit (commonly $1,000 to $10,000 per person). Coverage generally applies to expenses incurred within a set period, typically three years from the date of accident.

Clause 7 — Personal Injury Protection (PIP) In no-fault jurisdictions, PIP pays medical expenses, a percentage of lost wages, essential services, and death benefits regardless of who caused the accident. Benefit amounts, deductibles, and coordination with health insurance are governed by state statute and elected coverage tiers.

Clause 8 — Rental Reimbursement Coverage When the covered auto is disabled by a covered collision or comprehensive loss, this coverage reimburses rental costs up to a daily maximum (for example, $40 per day) and an aggregate maximum (for example, $1,200 per loss). Coverage begins after the loss is reported and ends when the vehicle is repaired, replaced, or the maximum is reached.

Clause 9 — Roadside Assistance / Towing and Labor This coverage pays for towing to the nearest qualified repair facility and for on-site labor such as jump-starts, tire changes, fuel delivery, and lockout service, up to the per-disablement limit shown on the Declarations Page. Labor must be performed at the place of disablement.

Clause 10 — Definition of Insured Persons An "insured" includes the Named Insured and resident spouse, family members related by blood, marriage, or adoption who reside in the household, and any person using the covered auto with permission within the scope of that permission. Coverage for permissive users is subject to the same terms and limits.

Clause 11 — Covered Autos and Newly Acquired Vehicles Covered autos include those listed on the Declarations Page, trailers owned by the insured, and newly acquired vehicles. A newly acquired vehicle receives automatic coverage broadest to the coverage the insured already carries, provided the Insurer is notified within 14 days (30 days for additional vehicles in some states).

Clause 12 — Policy Territory Coverage applies to accidents and losses occurring within the United States, its territories and possessions, Puerto Rico, and Canada. It also applies during transportation between their ports. Coverage does not extend to Mexico unless a specific Mexico endorsement is purchased.

Clause 13 — Deductible Application The applicable deductible is subtracted from each collision or comprehensive loss payment. Where a single event causes both collision and comprehensive damage, only one deductible — generally the higher — may apply, subject to state rules. Deductibles may be waived where the loss is caused by an identified, insured at-fault third party and subrogation is successful.

Clause 14 — Exclusion: Intentional Acts No coverage applies to bodily injury or property damage caused intentionally by, or at the direction of, an insured. This exclusion applies even if the actual injury or damage is different or greater than expected.

Clause 15 — Exclusion: Racing and Speed Contests The policy does not cover loss occurring while the covered auto is being used in, or in practice or preparation for, any prearranged or organized racing, speed, or demolition contest, or stunt activity.

Clause 16 — Exclusion: Commercial and Livery Use Losses occurring while the vehicle is used to carry persons or property for a fee — including delivery services and ride-share driving during passenger pickup or transport — are excluded unless a business-use or ride-share endorsement has been added.

Clause 17 — Exclusion: Non-Owned and Furnished Vehicles Coverage does not extend to vehicles furnished or available for the regular use of an insured but not declared on the policy, nor to vehicles owned by the insured that are not listed. This prevents insuring one vehicle while regularly driving another.

Clause 18 — Total Loss Settlement When repair costs plus salvage value exceed the vehicle's actual cash value, or exceed a state-defined total-loss threshold, the vehicle is declared a total loss. The Insurer pays the ACV determined by market valuation, less any applicable deductible, and takes ownership of the salvage.

Clause 19 — Subrogation Rights After paying a claim, the Insurer succeeds to the insured's right to recover from any responsible third party. The insured must do nothing to impair these rights and must cooperate in recovery efforts. Amounts recovered are applied first to reimburse the Insurer, with any deductible portion returned to the insured on a pro-rata basis.

Clause 20 — Duties After an Accident or Loss The insured must promptly notify the Company, cooperate in the investigation, submit to examination under oath if requested, provide proof of loss, protect the vehicle from further damage, and notify police when required (such as theft or hit-and-run). Failure to comply may reduce or void coverage for that loss.

Clause 21 — Cancellation and Nonrenewal The insured may cancel at any time by returning the policy or giving notice of the future cancellation date; earned premium is calculated pro rata. The Insurer may cancel for nonpayment of premium, material misrepresentation, or suspension/revocation of the driver's license of an insured, subject to statutory notice periods (commonly 10 days for nonpayment and 30–45 days for other reasons). Nonrenewal requires advance written notice as prescribed by state law.

Clause 22 — Anti-Theft and Safety Discounts Premium reductions apply for approved anti-theft devices, factory or aftermarket alarm systems, vehicle recovery systems, airbags, anti-lock brakes, and safe-driver telematics enrollment. Eligibility must be verified and documented; discounts are applied at issuance or renewal.

Clause 23 — Two or More Autos / Other Insurance If the policy covers two or more autos, the limits apply separately to each. When other collectible insurance applies to a loss, this policy pays only its share. For non-owned autos, this policy is excess over any other applicable coverage.

## Home Insurance Policies
- **Core Coverage**: Standard home policies cover dwelling, personal property, and general liability.
- **Dwelling Limit**: Default dwelling coverage is set at 100% of the home's estimated replacement cost.
- **Personal Property**: Covered up to 50% of the dwelling limit by default, unless upgraded via endorsement.
- **Water & Flood Exclusions**: Water damage from internal burst pipes is fully covered. However, flood damage is strictly excluded and requires a separate flood policy.
- **Standard Deductible**: The standard home deductible is $1,000 for all standard claims, excluding windstorm/hail endorsements.
- **Home Loan Policy Alignment (Mortgage Requirements)**:
  - Lenders/Banks require standard home insurance to cover 100% of the loan amount or the replacement cost of the structure.
  - Required Home Loan Insurance Documents:
    1. Certified Copy of the Insurance Binder showing the lender listed as the Loss Payee/Mortgagee.
    2. Paid-in-Full Receipt for the first year's premium (or evidence of Escrow account setup).
    3. Detailed Dwelling Coverage Declaration Page specifying fire, windstorm, and water pipe burst coverages.

## Life Insurance Policies
1. Term Life Insurance
Term Options: Term life policies are available in durations of 10, 20, and 30-year terms.
The selected term determines how long the policy remains in force, provided all required premiums are paid and the policy remains active.
The policy's effective date is the date from which coverage officially begins.
The policy's expiration date is determined by the selected term and policy effective date.
A customer may have only the term duration specified in their policy contract; agents should not assume that other term durations are available.
Demo Policy Rule: NorthBridge does not automatically provide a 25-year or 40-year term unless a specific product record confirms availability.
2. Policy Effective Date
The policy effective date is the date on which the life insurance coverage becomes active.
The effective date is important when determining the contestability period.
The effective date should be verified from the customer's policy record before providing date-specific information.
Agents should not assume that the application date and policy effective date are the same.
3. Contestability Period
Contestability Period: The contestability period is exactly 2 years from the policy effective date.
Claims made within this window are subject to verification of statements.
The existence of the contestability period does not automatically mean that a claim will be denied.
Claims should be reviewed according to the applicable policy terms and verification requirements.
Agents should avoid promising claim approval or denial during the contestability period.
The agent should verify the policy effective date before determining whether a claim falls inside or outside the two-year period.
4. Premium Payments
Premiums are the payments required to keep the life insurance policy active.
The customer's CRM record should be used to determine the actual premium amount applicable to that customer.
The Policy Handbook should be used for general premium-related rules.
Agents should not assume that all life insurance customers pay the same premium.
Premium frequency and payment amount should be verified against the customer's policy record.
5. Grace Period
Grace Period: A 31-day grace period applies to all missed premium payments before a policy officially lapses.
A missed payment does not mean the policy immediately lapses.
The customer should be informed that the grace period is 31 days.
The exact payment status should be verified using the customer's CRM/policy record.
Agents should not tell customers that coverage will continue indefinitely after a missed payment.
Once the applicable grace period expires, the policy may officially lapse according to the policy terms.
6. Policy Lapse
A policy may officially lapse after the applicable 31-day grace period when a required premium remains unpaid.
Agents should verify the customer's payment history before stating whether a policy has lapsed.
The Copilot should distinguish between:
Missed payment
Grace-period status
Active policy
Lapsed policy
The AI should not automatically assume that a missed payment means the policy has already lapsed.
7. Policy Status

Possible policy statuses in the NorthBridge CRM may include:

Active
Grace Period
Lapsed
Expired
Pending

The agent should always use the customer's actual CRM record when discussing the customer's current policy status.

8. Death Benefit / Face Value
The face value represents the policy's stated life insurance benefit.
The customer's actual face value must be obtained from the customer's policy record.
Agents should not assume that every term-life policy has the same face value.
For example, a customer's CRM record may show:
Policy: POL-LIFE-303
Policy Type: 20-Year Term Life
Face Value: $500,000
9. Beneficiary / Nominee
The beneficiary/nominee is the person or entity designated to receive the applicable life insurance benefit according to the policy terms.
The customer's beneficiary information should be treated as customer-specific information.
Agents should verify the customer's identity before accessing or modifying beneficiary information.
A beneficiary change should follow the applicable NorthBridge internal procedure.
Important: The current Policy Handbook does not specify the exact documentation or workflow required for changing a beneficiary/nominee.
Therefore, the Copilot must not invent a beneficiary-change procedure.
If the procedure is unavailable to the Level 1 agent, the request should be routed according to the appropriate internal workflow.
10. Customer Identity Verification
Customer-specific life insurance information should only be displayed after the customer's identity has been successfully verified.
The agent may verify the customer using an approved identifier such as:
CRM ID
Policy number
Full name
Phone number
Email
Once verified, the customer's information should remain associated with the active support session.
The agent should not have to repeatedly provide the CRM ID during the same call.
When the agent switches customers, the previous customer's context must be cleared.
11. Policy Renewal / Expiration
The policy's renewal or expiration information should be retrieved from the customer's policy record.
The agent should not calculate or guess a customer's expiration date without the required policy information.
When a customer asks about their policy expiration, the Copilot should prioritize CRM data.
12. Claims During the Contestability Period
Claims made within the 2-year contestability period are subject to verification of statements.
The Copilot should identify whether the claim potentially falls within the contestability period based on the policy effective date.
The Copilot should not make a final claim decision.
The agent should not promise approval or denial based solely on the contestability period.

## Commercial & Enterprise Policies
- **Commercial Property Policies**: Covers physical structures, business personal property, and operational equipment. Coverage forms include Basic, Broad, and Special Causes of Loss.
- **Business Income / Interruption**: Reimburses lost net income and continuing expenses (e.g., payroll) when operations are suspended due to a covered peril. Standard limits range from $100,000 to $2,000,000.
- **General Commercial Liability (GL)**: Protects businesses from claims of third-party bodily injury, property damage, and personal/advertising injury occurring on premises. Standard limit per occurrence is $1,000,000 with a $2,000,000 aggregate cap.
- **Commercial Auto Endorsements**: Covers non-owned and hired autos utilized during business operations. Requires special employee verification and state driving record audits.

## Cyber Liability Policies
- **First-Party Cyber Coverage**: Reimburses expenses related to forensic investigations, credit monitoring services for affected users, public relations response campaigns, and ransomware extortions.
- **Third-Party Cyber Liability**: Covers legal defense costs, regulatory settlement fines, and civil damages resulting from data privacy breaches or failures in network security.
- **Ransomware Deductibles**: Specialized cyber policies include a 24-hour waiting period deductible and require confirmation of offline system backups before ransom payouts are approved.

## Umbrella & Excess Liability Policies
- **Excess Indemnity Coverage**: Extends the liability limits of underlying primary auto, homeowner, or commercial liability policies. Standard minimum underlying policy limits are $250,000 per person / $500,000 per accident.
- **Self-Insured Retention (SIR)**: If a claim is covered by the umbrella policy but excluded by underlying primary policies, a self-insured retention limit (standardly $10,000) applies before coverage takes effect.

## Specialized Personal Endorsements (Home & Auto)
- **Scheduled Personal Property Endorsement**: Extends coverage for high-value items (e.g., jewelry, fine art, collectibles, silver) beyond standard policy sub-limits (which cap jewelry at $1,500). Scheduled items are covered up to appraised value with zero deductible.
- **Earthquake & Natural Perils Endorsement**: Standard homeowner policies exclude ground movement. Earthquake endorsements require a separate deductible (expressed as 5%, 10%, or 15% of the total dwelling limit).
- **Medical Payments (MedPay) for Auto**: Covers reasonable medical, dental, and funeral expenses for the insured and passengers injured in a vehicle accident, regardless of who is at fault. Standard limits range from $1,000 to $10,000.
